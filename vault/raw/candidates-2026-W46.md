### 1. An integrated single-cell and epigenomic resource for comparative analysis of the basal ganglia from BICAN
- 출처: europepmc · 2026-11-12 · **peer-reviewed**
- 저자: Zhang W, Ding W, Li K, et al.
- DOI/링크: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2026.101567

- 점수: 15 (키워드: genom, single-cell, multi-omic, epigenom, sequencing)

> The basal ganglia regulate motor, cognitive, and affective behaviors, and their dysfunction underlies diverse neurological and psychiatric disorders. Comprehensive, accessible multi-omics resources are needed to understand the regulatory mechanisms governing basal ganglia cell types. Here, we present an open, interactive web-based platform for exploring single-cell multi-omics datasets from basal ganglia, generated using 10X Multiome, single-nucleus methylome and chromatin conformation sequencing (snm3C-seq), and Paired-Tag technologies by the BICAN (NIH BRAIN Initiative Cell Atlas Network) consortium. The platform is available at https://basalganglia.epigenomes.net/and enables integrated visualization of gene expression, chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation, histone modifications, and chromatin conformation across cell types and human, macaque, marmoset, and mouse species, with direct genome browser support and comparative epigenomic functionality. Representative analyses demonstrate cell-type-specific regulatory landscapes, conserved and species-specific regulatory elements, and links between epigenomic regulation and transcription. This resource provides a scalable, community-oriented foundation for advancing basal ganglia biology and interpreting regulatory mechanisms relevant to brain function and disease.

### 2. CellSavior: an open-source framework for improved cell calling in targeted single-cell DNA sequencing
- 출처: europepmc · 2026-11-10 · **peer-reviewed**
- 저자: Kim T, Chung YS, Park D, et al.
- DOI/링크: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag808
- 코드/데이터: https://github.com/dauer-tgilab/CellSavior
- 점수: 13 (키워드: bioinformatic, genom, single-cell, sequencing)

> Motivation Single-cell DNA sequencing (scDNA-seq) has emerged as a key approach for studying complex genomic variation, including tumor heterogeneity. Despite its increasing commercial availability, exemplified by the Tapestri platform, analytical reproducibility remains limited due to reliance on proprietary and non-transparent pipelines. Among preprocessing steps, cell calling is particularly critical, as it determines which barcodes correspond to true cells rather than background noise and directly shapes all downstream analyses. However, existing methods typically rely on empirical thresholding and exhibit inconsistent performance across datasets. These limitations underscore the need for a robust and generalizable cell calling framework for reliable scDNA-seq analysis. Results We developed CellSavior, an open-source cell calling algorithm with an integrated downstream pipeline that produces standardized outputs. CellSavior employs an ensemble clustering strategy across multiple distance metrics, combined with confidence-weighted merging, to generate a consensus classification that reduces bias and improves robustness. Across 33 public and in-house single-cell DNA sequencing datasets, CellSavior recapitulated cell annotations generated by commercial software (CellFinder) in 31 cases (93.9%). In the remaining discordant cases, CellSavior yielded more biologically interpretable annotations, rescuing previously undetected clusters enriched for key hotspot genomic alterations. Availability The source code for CellSavior is freely available at https://github.com/dauer-tgilab/CellSavior; DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22744146. Supplementary information Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.

### 3. Pooled Multiplex Amplicon Massive Parallel Sequencing for High-Throughput Confirmation of Whole-Genome Sequencing Potentially Pathogenic Variants in Patients with Parkinsonism

- 코드/데이터: 초록에 링크 없음
- 점수: 13 (키워드: bioinformatic, genom, variant, sequencing)

> Whole-genome sequencing identifies large numbers of genetic variants, although only a subset requires independent confirmation for clinical interpretation. Sanger sequencing remains the standard confirmation method but is impractical for large-scale variant verification. We developed and evaluated a strategy for variant confirmation based on multi-amplicon massively parallel sequencing of pooled DNA samples. Whole-genome sequencing data were obtained from 86 individuals, including patients with parkinsonism and controls. Variants were selected using phenotype-driven filtering, ClinVar classification, and pathogenicity assessment criteria. A total of 307 variants were targeted using a custom amplicon panel comprising 290 amplicons. DNA samples were normalized, pooled into 16 mixtures, and sequenced following automated library preparation. Variant presence and sequencing quality were assessed using standard bioinformatic tools. Of the 307 selected variants, 243 were successfully confirmed. Unconfirmed variants included sequencing artifacts (18), insufficiently covered targets (5), large insertions or deletions exceeding 12 base pairs (36), and variants located within homopolymeric regions (5). Results for selected variants were concordant with Sanger sequencing, and observed variant frequencies corresponded to expected frequencies in pooled samples. This approach provides a robust, rapid, and cost-effective method for high-throughput genetic variants confirming identified by whole-genome sequencing while requiring only limited DNA input. Despite complete concordance with Sanger sequencing, larger validation studies are needed before multi-amplicon massively parallel sequencing can replace routine confirmatory testing. ### 4. Spatial multi-omic profiling of non-autonomous transcriptomic and epigenomic responses to genetic perturbation
- 출처: biorxiv · 2026-11-13 · **preprint** · bioinformatics
- 저자: Ghaemmaghami, J.; Lee, K. H.; Jiang, S.; et al.
- DOI/링크: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.12.766149
- 코드/데이터: 초록에 링크 없음
- 점수: 13 (키워드: genom, transcriptom, multi-omic, epigenom)

> Multicellular organisms develop and maintain differentiated cell states through a combination of cell-autonomous and non-autonomous regulatory mechanisms, including signal transduction pathways mediated by ligands, receptors, and kinases. Genetic perturbation through CRISPR pooled screening is widely used to study cell-autonomous regulatory mechanisms, but the challenges of cellular-resolution in situ measurement have limited the study of non-autonomous regulatory mechanisms. We developed Perturb-Tags, a spatial multi-omic technique for pooled CRISPR screening that tags nuclei with spatial barcodes, allowing joint measurement of single-nucleus gene expression, CRISPR guide RNAs, chromatin accessibility, and spatial positions within intact tissue. Perturb-Tags achieves cellular-resolution, unbiased gene expression and multi-omic profiling, and higher sensitivity than previous spatial perturbation approaches. We used Perturb-Tags to systematically perturb ligands, receptors, and all expressed kinases in human embryoid bodies. These experiments revealed extensive non-autonomous gene expression changes after perturbation, including distance-dependent loss of signaling pathway activity upon ligand knockdown, compensatory upregulation of alternative signaling pathways, and responses to receptor and kinase knockdown that vary with cell microenvironment. We also identified changes in the spatial organization of cells with ligand and kinase knockdowns. Finally, we trained a graph neural network to model the combined effects of genetic perturbations on gene expression and chromatin accessibility across an entire tissue. Our work provides a powerful experimental and computational platform for mechanistic studies of non-autonomous regulatory mechanisms across healthy and diseased tissues.

> Rare genetic disorders frequently remain undiagnosed despite the use of exome sequencing (ES) and genome sequencing (GS). This study aimed to enhance diagnostic yield by introducing a systematic framework: SMART filtering (Systematic, Multi-step, Automated, Rare variant prioritization Tool), designed to optimize GS analysis, with a particular emphasis on non-coding regions. Integrating such frameworks into clinical genomic pipelines can enhance variant interpretability and diagnostic performance, ultimately leading to better patient care outcomes.

- 점수: 12 (키워드: genom, single-cell, multi-omic, epigenom, proteom)

> Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a leading cause of kidney failure and excess morbidity and mortality. Once regarded as a uniform microvascular complication of chronic hyperglycaemia, DKD is now recognized as a biologically and clinically heterogeneous condition. Not all chronic kidney disease in individuals with diabetes can be attributed to diabetes, and distinguishing DKD from non-diabetic kidney disease (non-DKD) is essential for appropriate management. Multiple interacting pathways, including hyperglycaemia, haemodynamic stress, dyslipidaemia, obesity, inflammation and ancestry-specific genetic risk, drive diverse patterns of kidney injury and clinical trajectories. Conventional classification based on glomerular filtration rate or albuminuria alone fails to capture this complexity. Emerging multi-omics approaches are reshaping our understanding of DKD by identifying genomic, epigenomic, proteomic and metabolomic signatures that stratify risk, elucidate mechanisms and inform therapeutic response. Integration of these molecular layers with polygenic scores, data-driven clustering and single-cell kidney atlases is beginning to define disease endotypes and guide precision-medicine strategies. Large-scale collaborative efforts that combine deep phenotyping, biobanking and advanced analytics are accelerating the translation of molecular heterogeneity into actionable biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Despite ongoing challenges - including the need for validation, equitable representation and clinical implementation - omics-driven precision approaches offer the potential to transform DKD management by reducing residual risk and enabling more effective, precision interventions.

> Single-cell transcriptomic studies have revealed remarkable molecular heterogeneity across neuronal and glial populations during development, aging, and disease. > Background and ObjectivesDespite standard genetic testing, approximately two-thirds of patients with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) remain undiagnosed. Pathogenic noncoding and structural variants (SVs), that are difficult to detect or resolve using short-read sequencing, may contribute to this diagnostic gap. To evaluate the diagnostic contribution of long-read genome sequencing (lrGS), we recruited a cohort of 124 families with sporadic DEE who remained unsolved by prior ES. MethodsTrio lrGS (Oxford Nanopore or PacBio HiFi) was performed, complemented by short-read genome sequencing (srGS) and mRNA sequencing (blood or fibroblast) of index cases, where available. Comprehensive variant analysis included small nucleotide variants, indels, SVs, and short tandem repeats. ResultsWe established a diagnosis in 21 of 124 families (16.9%). Fourteen variants were retrospectively detectable in prior ES. The finding that two-thirds of diagnoses were retrospectively detectable in prior ES highlights the substantial value of systematic reanalysis. Further gains from genome sequencing may increasingly depend on improved interpretation of non-coding variation and integration of long-read-specific information. Our findings indicate that complex SVs represent the class for which lrGS currently yields the most distinctive diagnostic information.

### 16. ProClust provides reliable statistical significance of clusters for single-cell transcriptomic data
- 출처: biorxiv · 2026-11-10 · **preprint** · bioinformatics

- 점수: 11 (키워드: transcriptom, single-cell, single cell, sequencing)

> Clustering of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data is widely used to identify cell populations. However, overclustering can partition a single cell population into multiple clusters, potentially leading to spurious identification of cell subpopulations. Assessing the statistical significance of clusters is therefore needed to support their biological interpretation. We propose ProClust, a statistical test for clustering significance in scRNA-seq data. ProClust defines a single cluster as a log-concave distribution and adopts a projection-based approach to quantify clustering uncertainty through p-values. These projections can also provide interpretable visual evidence for individual clusterings.
